## Decision

**B_low_layer_summit_clear**: at least two of the airport's dawn reports show fog, visibility under 1500 m, or a broken/overcast ceiling at or below 1000 ft (about 300 m, well under the summit, which is ~563 m above the airport); no rain; and the model's 850 hPa humidity at 06:30 IST is below 90%, so the summit is more likely above the layer than inside it.

- A alone fires on monsoon stratus (23% of July dawns). On those days the photos show the summit inside the cloud. The 850 hPa term removes most of them (7% in July) and changes little in the fog season.
- C is the cleanest physically, but it rejects most of the mornings photographed as cloud seas (fog in two reports is rare even on good days), so the model would learn from very few positives.
- On the photo check, the airport part of B said 'sea' on 6 mornings and 5 of them were cloud seas; it found 5 of the 9 photographed cloud seas and never fired on the 6 socked-in or 3 partial mornings.

## What this label can't know

- **Cloud tops.** The airport sees the base of a layer, not its top. A thick layer can bury the summit (socked in); a thin one can show as patchy fog.
- **Valley fog the airport misses.** The airport is 15 km south-east of the hill. Fog in the valleys to the north and west can make a sea at Nandi while the airport is clear (2017-02-11 in the photo check is probably this).
- **Timing.** The window is 05:30-07:30 IST at half-hourly reports. Fog that forms after 07:30 or lifts before sunrise is miscounted.
- **The 850 hPa humidity is a model estimate,** one grid cell, not a measurement at the summit.
- **Haze vs cloud.** Thick haze over the plains can look like a sea in photos and is not a low layer in the reports.
- **The photo check is small** (29 mornings), labelled by one person by eye, mostly before 2021, and trusts each camera's clock. It's a sanity check, not a validation.

That gap is why the live app collects summit photos and grades every call against both the airport and the photos.
